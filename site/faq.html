Frequently Asked Questions

Two days on site for a standard build. Day one is preparation, which is grinding, crack repair and priming. Day two is the base coat, the flake broadcast and the clear coat. You walk on it the next day. Vehicles wait longer, and the exact date depends on the resin and the temperature in the building.

In Ontario a professional epoxy build is roughly $5 to $9 a square foot, flake around $6 to $10, polyaspartic around $6 to $12 and metallic around $8 to $12. A four hundred square foot double garage typically lands somewhere between about sixteen hundred and thirty two hundred dollars. Heavy preparation adds roughly one to three dollars a square foot. Those are market ranges. Yours depends on the concrete.

Because Chatham-Kent is a drained clay plain. The land is flat, the water table is managed by about four thousand kilometres of municipal drains, and the Brookston clay under your slab holds water against the concrete rather than letting it drain away. Concrete wicks that water upward. If it reaches the underside of a sealed coating, it pushes it off. Testing tells us which primer to use, and occasionally that a floor needs a drainage fix first.

Yes, with the right resin. Epoxy needs around ten degrees in the concrete and stalls under it. Polyaspartic keeps curing a long way below that. So in winter we read the concrete rather than the air, put heat in where the building needs it, and put polyaspartic on anything an epoxy build would be a bet on. We will tell you which one the conditions allow before you book.

The mildness is the reason. In January the average high in Chatham is around minus one and the average low around minus seven, and in February the average high is just above zero. So the slab crosses freezing and comes back day after day for three months. Water in the pores expands every time it does. A colder city freezes once and stays frozen, which is easier on concrete than cycling across zero all winter.

And they tell you to etch rather than grind, which never opens the concrete up, so there was no bond to begin with. Most of our repair work is stripping kits back off.

Yes, and square to the track. The door line is where salt and meltwater collect, so a floor that stops a foot short fails there first. Steps, landings and service door thresholds get coated too.

No, it is normal in farm buildings, packing rooms and wash bays. The coating is taken into the drain surround and coved up the wall so there is no joint at floor level for water to sit in. We do check the falls first, because a coating follows the shape underneath it and will not fix a floor that does not drain.

Professionally installed polyaspartic is generally quoted at fifteen to twenty years, some systems at twenty to twenty five. Epoxy is quoted at twenty or more. Preparation is the variable, not the resin. The clear coat wears first, and when it dulls at the tyre tracks it can be abraded and recoated without disturbing the build underneath.

Services We Install

Open any one for the build, the prep it needs and how it is looked after.

Flake Epoxy Flooring

A flake floor is three layers doing three different jobs. A pigmented resin base goes down first, coloured vinyl chip is thrown into it while it is still wet, and once that has hardened a clear wear coat is rolled over the whole thing.

View Flake Epoxy Flooring

Garage Floor Coating

A garage floor around here takes a beating that has very little to do with how carefully you park.

View Garage Floor Coating

Polyaspartic Floor Coating

Polyaspartic is a different family of resin to epoxy, and it solves two problems epoxy has.

View Polyaspartic Floor Coating

Basement Floor Coating

A basement is the easiest room in the house to coat and the easiest one to get wrong, and in Chatham-Kent it is moisture that decides which of the two you end up with.

View Basement Floor Coating

Concrete Floor Coating

Not every job is a garage. A good deal of what we coat is simply concrete that needs to stop being bare: a shop floor, a mechanical room, a storage building, a landing, a pad beside the lane.

View Concrete Floor Coating

Industrial and Commercial Flooring

A working floor is specified against three things: what rolls across it, how it gets cleaned, and when the building can afford to be shut. Everything else follows from those answers, which is why a retail unit, a warehouse, an equipment shop and a packing room all end up with different builds even though they start from the same materials.

View Industrial and Commercial Flooring

Patio and Outdoor Concrete Coating

An outdoor slab is not an indoor slab that happens to be outside. It has to handle daylight, standing water and a freeze-thaw cycle that runs all winter here, and it has a far shorter installation season. Use an indoor build outdoors and it will fail in the first spring.

View Patio and Outdoor Concrete Coating

Epoxy Floor Repair and Recoating

Peeling. Bubbling. Patches lifting where the tyres sit. A clear coat gone yellow by the window, or a floor that has simply turned dull and chalky.

View Epoxy Floor Repair and Recoating

Metallic Epoxy Flooring

Metallic is the decorative end of what we do. Metallic pigment is dispersed through clear resin and then moved while it is still wet, so the finished floor ends up with depth and movement running through it and reads closer to polished stone than to a coating.

View Metallic Epoxy Flooring
