About Chatham Epoxy Flooring

Why We Only Work In Chatham-Kent

Chatham-Kent is big. Wallaceburg to Ridgetown is most of an hour, and the municipality covers well over two thousand square kilometres. That is enough ground for one outfit.

Working one area means we know what the slabs are like here. We know which subdivisions were poured when. We know that a shop floor out on a concession road is going to be a different job to a garage on Keil Drive, and roughly how.

It also means when we say we will be there Tuesday, we are not driving in from two hours away.

What We Are Careful About

**We grind. We never acid etch.** Etching is faster and cheaper and it is why a great many floors in this area are peeling. Diamond grinding opens the concrete properly so the primer keys in mechanically.

**We test every slab for moisture before quoting.** This is drained clay country with a managed water table, and vapour coming up through a slab is the most common reason a coating fails here. The test costs nothing and it happens before there is a price on the table.

**We write the specification down.** Primer, base coat, broadcast rate, number of clear coats, cure times, colour, dates. If you are comparing quotes, you should be able to see exactly where two of them differ.

**We tell you when the answer is no.** Some slabs are too wet. Some are too far gone. Some floors would be better off with a cheaper build than the one you asked about. We would rather say so than take the deposit.

The Part Nobody Sees

Roughly two thirds of the labour on a resin floor happens before any resin comes out.

Emptying the room. Grinding the whole slab back to an open profile. Cutting out and filling cracks. Rebuilding spalled patches. Grinding out oil. Vacuuming continuously so the dust ends up in the machine and not in your house. Reading the moisture. Choosing the primer against that reading.

A quote that is dramatically cheaper than the others is almost always cheaper because that is the part being skipped. It does not show on day one. It shows in year two.

Nobody In The Middle

Flake Epoxy Flooring

A flake floor is three layers doing three different jobs. A pigmented resin base goes down first, coloured vinyl chip is thrown into it while it is still wet, and once that has hardened a clear wear coat is rolled over the whole thing.

Polyaspartic Floor Coating

Basement Floor Coating

Concrete Floor Coating

Not every job is a garage. A good deal of what we coat is simply concrete that needs to stop being bare: a shop floor, a mechanical room, a storage building, a landing, a pad beside the lane.

View Concrete Floor Coating

Industrial and Commercial Flooring

A working floor is specified against three things: what rolls across it, how it gets cleaned, and when the building can afford to be shut. Everything else follows from those answers, which is why a retail unit, a warehouse, an equipment shop and a packing room all end up with different builds even though they start from the same materials.

View Industrial and Commercial Flooring

Patio and Outdoor Concrete Coating

An outdoor slab is not an indoor slab that happens to be outside. It has to handle daylight, standing water and a freeze-thaw cycle that runs all winter here, and it has a far shorter installation season. Use an indoor build outdoors and it will fail in the first spring.

View Patio and Outdoor Concrete Coating

Epoxy Floor Repair and Recoating

Peeling. Bubbling. Patches lifting where the tyres sit. A clear coat gone yellow by the window, or a floor that has simply turned dull and chalky.

View Epoxy Floor Repair and Recoating

Metallic Epoxy Flooring

Metallic is the decorative end of what we do. Metallic pigment is dispersed through clear resin and then moved while it is still wet, so the finished floor ends up with depth and movement running through it and reads closer to polished stone than to a coating.
